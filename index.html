A hundred years of winning times

Three laps of the Piazza — roughly a kilometre. Times have crept down from about 1′26″ in the 1920s to the low 1′13″s, though the spread between a fast race and a slow one is as wide as it ever was. The record stands at 1′12″39, set on 2 July 2023.

Race week

Choosing the horses Trial runs Ritual & procession The race

Six trials (prove) precede every Palio: five spread over the three days after the horses are drawn — one each morning and evening — and the provaccia on the morning of the race itself. A contrada may swap its jockey right up to that morning; the horse, once drawn, can never be changed.

Il Drappellone — the only prize

There is no cup and no money. The winning contrada takes home a painted silk banner and keeps it forever, hung in its own museum. Siena calls it il cencio — the rag. A different artist paints one for each Palio, and since the 1970s the commission has gone to serious names: Renato Guttuso, Fernando Botero, Jim Dine, Milo Manara, Igor Mitoraj, Joe Tilson. The banner is presented in the Piazza on the eve of the race and raised on the judges' stand minutes before the start.
